How it works

Three simple steps

1

The screen scans

Options light up one by one on a timer. Nothing needs to be aimed at or pressed precisely — the screen does the work.

2

One signal selects

The camera watches for an eye gesture, or a facial change (such as raised eyebrows, or a downturned mouth). Whatever is highlighted at that moment is chosen.

3

Words become speech

Pick a predicted word or spell on the keyboard. BlinkTalk learns the words people really use — and speaks the finished sentence aloud.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is BlinkTalk really free?

Yes. BlinkTalk is free and open source — no ads, no subscriptions, no hidden costs. It was written for a friend with locked-in syndrome, and it is given away so that anyone who needs it can use it.

Who is BlinkTalk for?

It is for people with locked-in syndrome or other conditions who cannot use touch, a keyboard or their voice — and for the family members, carers and therapists who help them communicate.

What do we need to get started?

A Windows computer or an Android tablet or phone. The user communicates with whatever signal they can repeat — an eye or facial gesture — while the device camera is trained to spot the gesture, or a helper taps the screen, clicks the mouse, or presses the spacebar on a physical keyboard.

Can the user use it on their own?

Yes. Once the camera has been trained on their gesture, an eye or facial gesture is enough to select. A helper is needed only for the setup process when the app starts.
